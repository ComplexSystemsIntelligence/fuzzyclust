Fuzzy k-prototypes on mixed numeric and categorical data#

Records that mix measurements and categories, such as age and income next to occupation and region, are the rule in applications. Huang’s k-prototypes measures the numeric part by the squared Euclidean distance, the categorical part by the number of mismatches, and adds the two with a weight \(\gamma\): the prototype is the fuzzy mean of the numeric attributes together with the mode of every categorical one. With \(\gamma=0\) the method is fuzzy c-means on the numeric columns, with a sufficiently large \(\gamma\) it is fuzzy k-modes on the categorical columns; in between, both kinds of information shape the partition. Huang suggests the average standard deviation of the numeric attributes as the scale of \(\gamma\), and the implementation uses it as the default.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
from sklearn.metrics import adjusted_rand_score

from fuzzyclust import FCM
from fuzzyclust.plotting import PALETTE, plot_clusters
from fuzzyclust.relational import FuzzyKModes, FuzzyKPrototypes, make_mixed_blobs, plot_modes

mixed = make_mixed_blobs(n_samples=300, n_numeric=2, n_categorical=3, n_categories=3, n_clusters=3,
                         cluster_std=1.0, center_distance=3.0, noise=0.25, random_state=0)
X, y = mixed.data, mixed.target
print(X.head())
print("numeric centers:\n", mixed.centers, "\nplanted modes:\n", mixed.modes)

Three groups described by two measurements and three categories#

The numeric centers are three units apart with unit spread, so the groups overlap in the plane; each group also has a typical answer pattern in the three categorical columns, and every categorical entry is replaced by a random category with probability 0.25. Neither part alone separates the groups cleanly.

Fuzzy k-prototypes with the default weight#

Categorical columns are detected from the dtypes of the DataFrame; the default \(\gamma\) is the mean standard deviation of the numeric columns. The prototypes come back as a DataFrame with means and modes.

# [snippet:fit]
kp = FuzzyKPrototypes(n_clusters=3, n_init=5, random_state=0).fit(X)
print(f"gamma = {kp.gamma_:.2f}, categorical columns: {list(X.columns[kp.categorical_features_])}")
print(kp.prototypes_.round(2))
print(f"ARI = {adjusted_rand_score(y, kp.labels_):.2f}")
# [/snippet:fit]

Parameter study I: the weight gamma#

\(\gamma\) is scanned from zero (numeric only) to a value at which the three mismatches of the categorical part outweigh any numeric distance (categorical only). Both extremes are worse than the combination: the numeric part alone suffers from the overlap of the groups, the categorical part alone from the random answers, and a \(\gamma\) of the order of the numeric spread lets each part correct the errors of the other. The curves also show the two limits: at \(\gamma=0\) the partition equals that of FCM on the numeric columns, for large \(\gamma\) that of fuzzy k-modes on the categorical columns.

# [snippet:gamma_scan]
fcm = FCM(n_clusters=3, n_init=5, random_state=0).fit(X[["x1", "x2"]])
kmodes = FuzzyKModes(n_clusters=3, m=2.0, n_init=5, random_state=0).fit(X[["c1", "c2", "c3"]])
gammas = (0.0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 10.0, 100.0)
rows = []
for g in gammas:
    model = FuzzyKPrototypes(n_clusters=3, gamma=g, n_init=5, random_state=0).fit(X)
    rows.append((adjusted_rand_score(y, model.labels_), adjusted_rand_score(fcm.labels_, model.labels_),
                 adjusted_rand_score(kmodes.labels_, model.labels_)))
    print(f"gamma = {g:6.1f}: ARI = {rows[-1][0]:.2f}, agreement with FCM(numeric) = {rows[-1][1]:.2f}, "
          f"with k-modes(categorical) = {rows[-1][2]:.2f}")
# [/snippet:gamma_scan]
rows = np.asarray(rows)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 3.8))
xg = np.arange(len(gammas))
ax.plot(xg, rows[:, 0], "o-", color=PALETTE[0], label="ARI with the true groups")

Parameter study II: fuzziness, restarts and new records#

The exponent \(m\) softens the partition as usual. Restarts guard against the local optima of random prototype starts; on these data all starts reach the same objective, and the restarts cost little. predict_proba assigns new records, including records with a category never seen in training, which counts as one mismatch with every mode.

for m in (1.5, 2.0, 3.0):
    model = FuzzyKPrototypes(n_clusters=3, m=m, n_init=5, random_state=0).fit(X)
    print(f"m = {m:.1f}: ARI = {adjusted_rand_score(y, model.labels_):.2f}, "
          f"mean max membership = {model.membership_.max(axis=1).mean():.2f}")
single = [FuzzyKPrototypes(n_clusters=3, random_state=s).fit(X).objective_ for s in range(5)]
print(f"objective of five single runs: {np.round(single, 1)}; best of five restarts: {kp.objective_:.1f}")
new = X.iloc[:3].copy()
new.iloc[0, 2] = "unseen"
print(kp.predict_proba(new).round(3))

Take-away. Fuzzy k-prototypes clusters mixed records without forcing the categories into a numeric geometry: means for measurements, modes for categories, memberships from the combined dissimilarity. The weight \(\gamma\) decides how much a mismatch counts against a unit of squared numeric distance and interpolates between fuzzy c-means on the numeric part and fuzzy k-modes on the categorical part. A value of the order of the numeric spread (Huang’s rule and the default) lets the two kinds of attributes correct each other and beats either alone.
