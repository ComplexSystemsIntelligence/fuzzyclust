Robust and shape-oriented variants of fuzzy c-means
---------------------------------------------------

Fuzzy c-means assumes compact, roughly spherical clusters and is pulled
away by outliers. These examples relax one assumption at a time: the
possibilistic c-means (absolute typicalities, their scale parameters and the
coincident-cluster failure), the possibilistic fuzzy c-means (memberships
and typicalities with the a/b trade-off), noise clustering (a virtual noise
cluster at constant distance δ), kernel fuzzy c-means (rings and moons with
a kernel-width scan), fuzzy c-varieties and c-elliptotypes (lines as
prototypes on crossing and collinear segments, compared with GK) and fuzzy
c-shells (circles as prototypes on concentric and intersecting circles with
radius recovery), volume prototypes with adaptive merging, and feature
weighting (weighted FCM and SCAD) that learns which variables matter.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A ring has no interior points, so a point prototype placed at its center is far from every member, and FCM cannot tell concentric rings apart at all. Shell clustering describes cluster i by a center \mathbf{v}_i and a radius r_i and measures the distance of a sample as its deviation from the circle. The fuzzy c-spherical shells algorithm (FCSS) of Krishnapuram, Nasraoui and Frigui uses the algebraic distance (\|\mathbf{x}_k-\mathbf{v}_i\|^2-r_i^2)^2, which is quadratic in a reparametrized prototype and therefore has a closed-form weighted least-squares update; Davé&#x27;s original fuzzy c-shells (FCS) uses the radial distance (\|\mathbf{x}_k-\mathbf{v}_i\|-r_i)^2 and needs a Newton iteration per cluster. The example recovers the radii of concentric and of intersecting circles, compares the two algorithms, and shows why the initialization matters.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Fuzzy c-means treats every feature alike: after standardization a feature of pure noise contributes as much to the distance as a feature that separates the clusters, and enough such features drown the structure. Two extensions learn feature weights together with the partition. The weighted FCM of Keller and Klawonn uses one weight vector \boldsymbol\alpha for all clusters, D^2_{ik}=\sum_j\alpha_j^t(x_{kj}-v_{ij})^2, and SCAD (simultaneous clustering and attribute discrimination) of Frigui and Nasraoui gives every cluster its own weights w_{ij}. In both methods a feature receives a large weight when the members of the clusters scatter little along it: a noise feature, along which every cluster is as wide as the data, is down-weighted. This example adds noise features to three groups, compares the learned weights, and shows SCAD on clusters that live in different subspaces.">
