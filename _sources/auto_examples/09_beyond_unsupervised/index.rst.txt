Beyond a single unsupervised run
--------------------------------

Fuzzy clustering that uses more than one data matrix and one random start:
partial supervision by a few class labels (Pedrycz--Waletzky) and by
must-link / cannot-link pairs (pairwise-constrained competitive
agglomeration), co-clustering of the rows and columns of a document--term
matrix (FCCM, Fuzzy CoDoK), collaborative clustering of the same objects
at several data sites that exchange only partition matrices, and
consensus clustering of a library of soft partitions (co-association
matrix, consensus functions, evidence accumulation and the lifetime
criterion for the number of clusters).

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="A document--term matrix has two kinds of entities, documents (rows) and terms (columns), and a topic is a pair: a group of documents and the vocabulary that characterizes it. Fuzzy co-clustering assigns memberships to both: u_{ik} of document k and w_{ij} of term j in co-cluster i. FCCM (Oh, Honda and Ichihashi) maximizes the aggregation degree \sum_{ikj}u_{ik}w_{ij}r_{kj} regularized by the entropies of both membership sets; Fuzzy CoDoK (Kummamuru, Dhawale and Krishnapuram) uses quadratic regularizers instead. Neither needs a distance between sparse count vectors. The data are a synthetic corpus: four topics with vocabularies of different size, Poisson counts that are high inside a topic block and low elsewhere, plus twenty general terms that occur everywhere at the background rate. Rows and columns are shuffled, so the blocks are invisible until the co-clusters reorder the matrix.">

A cluster ensemble fits a library of such runs and combines them. Soft partitions are combined through the co-association matrix: the library average of the similarity of two objects&#x27; membership vectors, which needs no matching of cluster labels between the members and works even when the members have different numbers of clusters. A consensus function then partitions the objects from this matrix: fuzzy c-means on its rows, spectral partitioning, evidence accumulation (hierarchical clustering of 1-s_{kl}), the soft meta-clustering algorithm sMCLA or information-theoretic k-means (Punera and Ghosh; Strehl and Ghosh; Fred and Jain).">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Fred and Jain&#x27;s evidence accumulation turns the choice of the number of clusters into a property of the co-association matrix. The members of the library deliberately over-segment the data (many more clusters than expected, a different number for each member); objects of the same natural group stay together in most members, objects of different groups rarely. Hierarchical clustering of the distance 1-s_{kl} then gives a dendrogram, and the chosen number of clusters is the one that persists over the widest range of thresholds, that is, the one with the longest lifetime. Neither the members nor the consensus needs to know the number of groups.">
