------------------------------------

data, choosing the number of clusters and working with pandas / scikit-learn.
Also: generalized fuzzifiers and deterministic annealing, subtractive and
mountain clustering, compatible cluster merging and stability-based

Robust and shape-oriented variants of fuzzy c-means
---------------------------------------------------

Fuzzy c-means assumes compact, roughly spherical clusters and is pulled
away by outliers. These examples relax one assumption at a time: the
possibilistic c-means (absolute typicalities, their scale parameters and the
coincident-cluster failure), the possibilistic fuzzy c-means (memberships
and typicalities with the a/b trade-off), noise clustering (a virtual noise
cluster at constant distance δ), kernel fuzzy c-means (rings and moons with
a kernel-width scan), fuzzy c-varieties and c-elliptotypes (lines as
prototypes on crossing and collinear segments, compared with GK) and fuzzy
c-shells (circles as prototypes on concentric and intersecting circles with
radius recovery), volume prototypes with adaptive merging, and feature
weighting (weighted FCM and SCAD) that learns which variables matter.

Graph-based fuzzy clustering
----------------------------

Clusters of arbitrary shape found through a graph over the samples: the
hybrid minimal spanning tree / Gath–Geva algorithm (inconsistent-edge
cutting, volume-based splitting, GG refinement, validation of the number of
clusters and similarity of the resulting fuzzy clusters) and fuzzy c-medoid
clustering with geodesic distances on a k-nearest-neighbour graph, compared
with fuzzy c-means and with clustering of the Isomap projection on rings,
moons, spirals and the S-curve.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Prototype-based algorithms describe a cluster by one geometric object and fail when the groups have arbitrary shapes. Graph-theoretic methods start instead from the minimal spanning tree (MST) of the data and obtain clusters by removing edges. Zahn&#x27;s classical criteria for choosing these edges compare an edge with the mean edge length of the whole tree (global threshold) or with the edges in its own neighborhood (local inconsistency). Both find well-separated groups, but a sparse chain of points between two groups keeps them together: the chaining effect of single linkage. The hybrid MST–GG algorithm of Vathy-Fogarassy, Feil, and Abonyi adds a third criterion. A subtree is split at the edge that minimizes the total fuzzy hypervolume \sum_j\sqrt{\det\mathbf{F}_j} of the resulting parts, which breaks chains and bends because a union of two compact groups has a far larger volume than its parts. Finally, the crisp tree partition initializes Gath–Geva clustering, which is too sensitive to its starting point to be run from a random partition on such data but refines a good start into a fuzzy, parametric description (centers, covariances, memberships).">

Relational, categorical and mixed data
--------------------------------------

Fuzzy clustering when the objects are not feature vectors or the mean is
meaningless: relational fuzzy c-means as the exact dual of FCM on a matrix

β-spread (NERF), fuzzy c-medoids on dynamic-time-warping dissimilarities of
time series with the linear-complexity candidate search, fuzzy k-modes on
categorical survey data, fuzzy k-prototypes on mixed records with the
weight γ, Gower's coefficient as a bridge between mixed data and the
relational algorithms, and autocorrelation-based clustering of time series.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Fuzzy c-means needs the data as vectors: a prototype is a weighted mean and the distance to it is Euclidean. Hathaway, Davenport and Bezdek showed that the same functional can be minimized when only the matrix \mathbf{R} of squared pairwise distances is known. The prototype becomes a weight vector over the objects, \mathbf{v}_i=\mathbf{U}_i^m/\sum_k\mu_{ik}^m, and the squared distance of object k to it is d^2_{ik}=(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{v}_i)_k-\tfrac12\mathbf{v}_i^T\mathbf{R}\mathbf{v}_i, an identity that holds for any weights summing to one as long as \mathbf{R} is Euclidean. Relational FCM (RFCM) therefore reproduces FCM exactly, iterate by iterate, when \mathbf{R} comes from Euclidean data. When it does not (squared city-block distances, cubed distances, dynamic-time-warping dissimilarities), some d^2_{ik} turn negative and RFCM breaks down. NERF c-means (Hathaway and Bezdek) adds a constant \beta to all off-diagonal entries, which shifts every relational distance by \tfrac{\beta}{2}\lVert\mathbf{e}_k-\mathbf{v}_i\rVert^2; it raises \beta adaptively, only when a negative distance appears and only as much as needed.">

Incomplete, very large and image data
--------------------------------------

Fuzzy c-means meets three realities of data that the textbook setting
leaves out. Values are *missing*: the four strategies of Hathaway and
Bezdek (whole data, partial distances, optimal completion, nearest
prototype) are compared across missingness rates, and the imputation that
optimal completion delivers is examined. Data are *very large* or arrive
as a *stream*: random sample and extend, single-pass and online FCM are
compared with the literal FCM on 200 000 points in time and agreement, and
a drifting stream is processed chunk by chunk with ``partial_fit``. The
data are an *image*: spatially constrained variants (FCM_S, FCM_S1/S2,
EnFCM, FGFCM, FLICM) are compared with pixel-wise FCM under increasing
Gaussian and salt-and-pepper noise, and the neighborhood weight and window
size are studied.

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Fuzzy c-means needs a complete data matrix: a distance between a sample and a prototype is a sum over all features. When values are missing (``NaN``), Hathaway and Bezdek (2001) distinguish four strategies. The whole-data strategy (WDS) drops every incomplete sample. The partial-distance strategy (PDS) keeps all samples and measures the distance over the observed features only, rescaled to the full dimension, D^2_{ik}=\frac{n}{I_k}\sum_j I_{kj}(x_{kj}-v_{ij})^2. The optimal completion strategy (OCS) treats the missing values as additional variables of the c-means functional and updates them to the membership-weighted mean of the prototype coordinates, so that the data are imputed while they are clustered. The nearest-prototype strategy (NPS) is its hard version: a missing value is copied from the nearest prototype. This example plants values missing completely at random into well-separated Gaussian clusters and asks how far each strategy&#x27;s prototypes drift from those of FCM on the complete data as the missing rate grows.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Literal fuzzy c-means touches every sample in every iteration and keeps the N\times c partition in memory. Havens, Bezdek, Leckie, Hall and Palaniswami (2012) compared three ways around this. Random sample and extend (rseFCM) clusters a random sample and computes the memberships of all samples in one pass from the fixed prototypes. Single-pass FCM (spFCM) walks through the data in chunks and carries the prototypes of a chunk, weighted by the membership mass they collected, into the next chunk as c heavy samples of weighted FCM. Online FCM (oFCM) clusters every chunk independently and clusters the weighted chunk prototypes at the end. The paper measures the speedup over literal FCM and the agreement of the hardened partitions with the literal FCM partition. This example repeats the experiment on 200 000 two-dimensional samples of five clusters of unequal size; the smallest holds only 5 % of the data, which makes a small random sample risky.">

Both summaries treat the stream as stationary: an observation from the first chunk counts as much as one from the last. When the clusters drift, the summary lags behind. This example sends a stream of 40 chunks through both algorithms while the three cluster centers move along straight lines, and measures the distance of the model&#x27;s prototypes from the current true centers. Pixel-wise FCM labels every pixel on its own, so noise that pushes a pixel&#x27;s value across the midpoint between two gray levels produces a wrong label, however clear the surrounding region is. The spatially constrained variants add the neighborhood of every pixel to the functional: FCM_S (Ahmed et al. 2002) adds the distances of the neighbors to the prototype, FCM_S1 and FCM_S2 (Chen and Zhang 2004) replace them by a mean- or median-filtered image computed once, EnFCM (Szilágyi et al. 2003) clusters the gray-level histogram of a linearly smoothed image, FGFCM (Cai, Chen and Zhang 2007) does the same with locally adaptive weights, and FLICM (Krinidis and Chatzis 2010) adds a parameter-free fuzzy local factor. Beyond a single unsupervised run
--------------------------------

Fuzzy clustering that uses more than one data matrix and one random start:
partial supervision by a few class labels (Pedrycz--Waletzky) and by
must-link / cannot-link pairs (pairwise-constrained competitive
agglomeration), co-clustering of the rows and columns of a document--term
matrix (FCCM, Fuzzy CoDoK), collaborative clustering of the same objects
at several data sites that exchange only partition matrices, and
consensus clustering of a library of soft partitions (co-association
matrix, consensus functions, evidence accumulation and the lifetime
criterion for the number of clusters).
